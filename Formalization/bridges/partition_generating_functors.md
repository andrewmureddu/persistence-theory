# Partition-Generating Functors for No-Partition Systems

*Status: candidate bridge. This note does not extend A.20 as a theorem. It isolates the typing problem exposed by WI-E.3 and the historical blind-spot audit: some systems may satisfy ACP/CDT at the unpartitioned level while lacking a primitive subsystem partition needed for A.20. The bridge asks when the missing partition can be generated by a canonical operation.*

*Companion documents: `bridges/restraint_power.md`, `reductions/multiscale_rg.md`, `research/blind_spot_atlas_2026-04-24.md`, `research/historical_blind_spot_audit_2026-04-24.md`.*

## 1. Motivation

A.20 assumes a subsystem partition

$$
M_I = M_1 \oplus \cdots \oplus M_N
$$

before it defines subsystem capacities $C_i$, coupling capacities $C_{ij}$, and concentrations $\gamma_i$. That is fine for systems whose decomposition is given by the domain: species in an ecosystem, departments in an organization, firms in a market, syndrome/code sectors in a quantum code, or two MASAs generated by conjugate observables.

WI-E.3 asks what happens when no such natural partition is available: critical matter, all-to-all neural networks, scale-free Gaussian fields, or strongly correlated systems whose relevant structure is not visible at the native level.

The historical audit suggests a possibility. "No natural partition" may mean "no primitive partition at the current scale." The missing object may be a partition-generating operation:

$$
\Pi : X \mapsto (M_1(X), \ldots, M_N(X)).
$$

This note makes that type signature precise and records the first mathematical constraints.

## 2. The Typing Problem

Let $X$ be an ACP system for which the broad CDT objects are typed:

- a state space $\Omega_X$;
- a macrostate space $M_X$;
- a transition kernel $P_X(m'|m)$ or induced dynamics;
- a conditional entropy, rank, accessible-mode, or equivalent future-bearing functional;
- dissolution and crystallization boundaries.

Call $X$ **A.20-untyped** when these objects are available but no domain-native subsystem partition has been selected.

The question is not whether A.20 is false for $X$. The question is whether A.20 is undefined until an additional structure is supplied.

## 3. Partition-Generating Functor

Let $\mathsf{Sys}$ be a category of ACP systems and structure-preserving maps. A morphism $f : X \to Y$ should at minimum preserve the transition structure relevant to ACP: coarse-graining, kernel conjugacy, or a specified weaker equivalence.

Let $\mathsf{Part}$ be the category whose objects are finite decompositions of macrostate or internal spaces and whose morphisms are refinement/coarsening maps or block-respecting isomorphisms.

***Definition 3.1 (Partition-generating functor).*** A partition-generating functor on a class $\mathsf{C} \subseteq \mathsf{Sys}$ is an assignment

$$
\Pi : \mathsf{C} \to \mathsf{Part}
$$

such that:

1. **Typing:** $\Pi(X)$ is a decomposition of the A.20-relevant internal space of $X$:
   $$
   \Pi(X) = (M_1(X), \ldots, M_N(X)).
   $$
2. **Equivariance:** for every isomorphism $f : X \to Y$ in $\mathsf{C}$,
   $$
   \Pi(Y) = f_\* \Pi(X),
   $$
   up to relabeling of blocks.
3. **A.20 admissibility:** the induced blocks make subsystem capacities $C_i$, coupling capacities $C_{ij}$, and total capacity $C_{\mathrm{tot}}$ well-defined.
4. **Nontriviality:** for the target application, $\Pi(X)$ is neither the indiscrete one-block partition nor the discrete partition into meaningless atomic labels, unless the theory explicitly identifies one of those as the correct boundary case.

The equivariance condition is the mathematical version of "not arbitrary." It says the generated partition must be determined by structure in $X$, not by coordinates, labels, or the researcher's preference.

## 4. First Constraint: Symmetry Blocks Canonical Partitions

The first theorem is a counterexample generator.

***Theorem 4.1 (Symmetry obstruction).*** Let $X$ be a finite A.20-untyped system whose automorphism group $\mathrm{Aut}(X)$ acts primitively on the candidate internal degrees of freedom $M_I$. Then no nontrivial partition $\Pi(X)$ can be invariant under all automorphisms of $X$.

In particular, if $\mathrm{Aut}(X)$ is the full symmetric group on $M_I$, every equivariant partition selector returns either the one-block partition or the partition into singletons.

*Proof.* A partition of $M_I$ invariant under $\mathrm{Aut}(X)$ is exactly a block system for the group action. By definition, a primitive group action admits no nontrivial block systems: the only invariant partitions are the indiscrete partition and the discrete singleton partition. The full symmetric group acts primitively, so the special case follows. ■

***Corollary 4.2 (No universal canonical $\Pi$).*** There is no partition-generating functor that assigns a nontrivial canonical A.20 partition to every finite ACP system while remaining equivariant under all system isomorphisms.

*Proof.* Apply Theorem 4.1 to any system whose internal degrees are fully symmetric under $\mathrm{Aut}(X)$. A universal functor would have to assign a nontrivial invariant partition there, contradicting primitivity. ■

This is the first useful mathematical result. A.20 cannot demand a nontrivial primitive partition in every possible system. Either the domain supplies a partition, the dynamics generate one by breaking symmetry, or A.20 remains untyped.

## 5. Second Constraint: Structure Can Generate Partitions

The obstruction above is not fatal. It says only that partitions cannot be generated from no structure. If $X$ carries a nontrivial operator, graph, covariance, kernel, or scale flow, that structure can break the symmetry.

***Definition 5.1 (Selector datum).*** A selector datum for an A.20-untyped system $X$ is a domain-native object

$$
S_X
$$

constructed from the ACP-relevant dynamics of $X$, such as:

- a coupling graph or weighted adjacency matrix;
- a covariance or precision matrix;
- a transfer operator;
- an RG coarse-graining map;
- a mutual-information matrix;
- a spectral decomposition of the transition kernel.

The selector datum is admissible only if it is invariantly attached to $X$: isomorphic systems carry conjugate selector data.

***Definition 5.2 (Stable selector).*** A selector datum $S_X$ is stable when it has a nondegenerate structural feature that supports a block decomposition: an eigengap, a clustering gap, a modularity gap, a scale separation, or a distinguished invariant subspace.

***Proposition 5.3 (Spectral partition generation under a simple gap).*** Let $X$ be a finite A.20-untyped system with a self-adjoint selector operator $S_X$ on $M_I$. Suppose $S_X$ has a simple isolated eigenvalue $\lambda$ separated from the rest of the spectrum by a gap $\delta > 0$. Then the associated one-dimensional eigenspace $E_\lambda(X)$ is canonically generated and equivariant under isomorphism.

If the coordinates of an eigenvector $v_\lambda$ in the candidate internal basis are separated from zero and both signs occur, the sign partition

$$
M_+(X) = \{a : v_\lambda(a) > 0\}, \qquad
M_-(X) = \{a : v_\lambda(a) < 0\}
$$

is a nontrivial equivariant partition, up to swapping the two labels.

*Proof.* For a self-adjoint operator, a simple isolated eigenvalue determines a unique one-dimensional spectral projector

$$
P_\lambda = \frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_\Gamma (zI-S_X)^{-1}\,dz
$$

where $\Gamma$ encloses $\lambda$ and no other spectral value. If $f : X \to Y$ is an isomorphism, admissibility of the selector datum gives $S_Y = f S_X f^{-1}$, hence

$$
P_\lambda(Y) = f P_\lambda(X) f^{-1}.
$$

So the eigenspace is equivariant. Choosing an eigenvector is unique up to sign. If no coordinate is zero and both signs occur, the positive and negative support sets are therefore well-defined up to simultaneous sign flip, which swaps labels but preserves the nontrivial partition. If the eigenvector is one-signed in the chosen internal basis, the selector has produced a distinguished mode but not a subsystem partition. ■

***Remark 5.4 (Why the gap matters).*** Without a spectral gap, small perturbations can rotate the eigenspace or merge it with nearby modes. Then the "generated partition" is an artifact of coordinates or numerical noise. The gap is the restraint mechanism for the selector itself.

***Corollary 5.5 (Partition generation is conditional).*** A.20 can be extended to an A.20-untyped system by a selector $\Pi_{S}$ only under named stability hypotheses on $S_X$. The hypotheses are not bookkeeping; they are the mathematical content that distinguishes a generated partition from an arbitrary one.

## 6. Relation to Multi-Scale RG

Appendix A.18 already supplies one family of selector data: a scale tower

$$
\sigma_1 : \Omega \to M_1,\quad \sigma_2 : M_1 \to M_2,\quad \ldots,\quad \sigma_L : M_{L-1} \to M_L.
$$

In this setting, a partition need not exist at the finest level. It may appear only after coarse-graining:

$$
\Pi_\ell(X) := \text{block structure induced on } M_\ell \text{ by } \sigma_\ell \circ \cdots \circ \sigma_1.
$$

At a critical point, the RG flow may generate scale-invariant blocks, relevant/irrelevant directions, or spectral sectors. That is precisely the historical pattern from critical phenomena: the regularity was visible before the transformation vocabulary was available.

***Conjecture 6.1 (RG-generated A.20 typing).*** For critical systems satisfying the A.18 scale-tower hypotheses and possessing a nontrivial RG fixed point with stable relevant directions, A.20 becomes typed on the relevant/irrelevant decomposition generated by the RG linearization:

$$
D R_{H^\*} : T_{H^\*}\mathcal{H} \to T_{H^\*}\mathcal{H}.
$$

The A.20 partition is not primitive in the microscopic system; it is generated by the tangent-space decomposition of the RG flow near the fixed point.

*Status:* conjectural. This is the mathematically cleanest version of the WI-E.3 no-partition response, but it requires a full compatibility proof between A.18's scale tower and A.20's coordination-capacity blocks.

## 7. Minimal Test Cases

### 7.1 Fully Symmetric Finite System

Let $M_I = \{1,\ldots,n\}$ and let the transition or coupling structure be invariant under all permutations. Then $\mathrm{Aut}(X) = S_n$ and Theorem 4.1 applies.

Expected result:

- no nontrivial canonical $\Pi$ exists;
- CDT may still be typed through the unpartitioned transition entropy;
- A.20 remains undefined unless an external or dynamical perturbation breaks symmetry.

This is the toy counterexample every proposed $\Pi$ must pass.

### 7.2 Two-Community Weighted Graph

Let $X$ have a coupling matrix

$$
W =
\begin{pmatrix}
a(\mathbf{1}_{p\times p}-I_p) & b\mathbf{1}_{p\times q}\\
b\mathbf{1}_{q\times p} & a(\mathbf{1}_{q\times q}-I_q)
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad a>b>0.
$$

The graph Laplacian has a Fiedler vector separating the two communities. When the eigengap is positive, Proposition 5.3 generates the partition. A.20 can then be applied to the two blocks.

Expected result:

- $\Pi$ exists and is equivariant;
- $\Pi$ fails continuously as $a-b \to 0$;
- the failure point coincides with restoration of symmetry and loss of nontrivial canonical partition.

*Smoke check.* `simulations/partition_selector_toy.py` implements this finite test with a dependency-free Jacobi eigensolver. With the default parameters `(p,q,a,b)=(4,4,1.0,0.2)`, the Fiedler selector recovers `[0,1,2,3] | [4,5,6,7]`. On the fully symmetric comparison graph, the Fiedler eigenspace is degenerate and the script selects no partition.

### 7.3 Scale-Free Gaussian Field

Let $X$ be a Gaussian field with covariance operator $C(k) \sim |k|^{-\alpha}$. A primitive local subsystem split may be noncanonical at criticality, but a Fourier or RG decomposition can generate scale sectors.

Expected result:

- CDT can be stated in terms of entropy/rank of the full covariance or precision operator;
- A.20 requires a generated partition into scale, momentum, or relevant/irrelevant sectors;
- the generated partition depends on the chosen selector datum, so invariance across reasonable selectors becomes the key test.

## 8. What This Bridge Does and Does Not Claim

**Claimed here:**

- A.20 has an explicit typing dependence on a subsystem partition.
- No universal nontrivial canonical partition exists for all systems; symmetry obstructs it.
- Nontrivial partitions can be generated when the system carries stable selector data.
- The no-partition problem should be reformulated as a search for admissible selector data and stability hypotheses.

**Not claimed here:**

- that every critical system admits an A.20 partition;
- that spectral or RG selectors are always physically correct;
- that generated partitions are unique across selector families;
- that A.20 has been extended to Type II / Type III operator algebras;
- that CDT depends on A.20 typing.

The important separation is:

$$
\text{CDT may apply to } X
\quad \text{while} \quad
\text{A.20 applies only to } (X,\Pi(X)).
$$

## 9. New Open Problem

**OP-19: Partition-generating functors for A.20.**

Characterize classes of ACP systems for which there exists an admissible partition-generating functor

$$
\Pi : \mathsf{C} \to \mathsf{Part}
$$

that is equivariant under system isomorphisms, stable under perturbations, and sufficient to type A.20's coordination-capacity decomposition.

Subproblems:

1. Classify selector data $S_X$ that are natural for ACP systems: kernels, covariances, precision matrices, mutual-information operators, RG maps, or correlation graphs.
2. Prove stability conditions under which $S_X$ generates a nontrivial partition.
3. Identify obstruction classes where $\mathrm{Aut}(X)$ is primitive and no nontrivial canonical partition exists.
4. Determine whether A.20 conclusions are invariant across distinct admissible selectors.
5. Relate RG-generated partitions to A.18's scale tower and to OP-RP-8 partition invariance.

## 10. Recommendation

The next mathematical move should be finite and surgical:

1. Formalize $\mathsf{Sys}_{\mathrm{fin}}$, the category of finite ACP Markov systems with kernel-preserving isomorphisms.
2. Define selector data from the transition kernel:
   $$
   S_X(a,b) = I(a_t; b_{t+\Delta t}) \quad \text{or} \quad S_X = \frac{1}{2}(P_X + P_X^\top).
   $$
3. Prove the symmetry obstruction and spectral selector proposition in that concrete category.
4. Test the two-community graph and fully symmetric graph.
5. Only then ask whether the construction survives RG / continuum limits.

This is the mathematician's first move: make the type signature unforgiving, prove the obstruction, prove the first conditional construction, and let the framework earn its next abstraction.
