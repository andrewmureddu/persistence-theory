# Cross-Domain Generativity Protocol

*Status: methodological bridge. This note is not a new theorem and does not add a reduction. It operationalizes the A.21 self-application result: if ACP is a living unification, it must use incomplete cross-domain mappings as instruments for generating disciplined successor questions.*

*Companion documents: `bridges/generativity_criterion.md`, `proofs/meta_theoretic_coherence_theorem.md`, `WHAT_IF.md`, `research/blind_spot_atlas_2026-04-24.md`.*

---

## 1. Motivation

The A.21 result says that a correct self-representing ACP cannot be finished. For productive theory evolution, $G_t(T) > 1$ is the local adaptive coherence steering condition: progress must open well-posed inquiry faster than it closes uncertainty.

That changes how cross-domain inspiration should be handled. Unexpected domains are not merely a source of analogies. They are probes. A domain that only confirms the existing vocabulary adds little. A domain that partially maps, fails to map, or forces a missing observable into view can improve the theory by revealing where its present boundary lies.

The incompleteness is therefore functional. The thing that must remain unfinished is also the thing that helps the program see.

## 2. Intake Objects

Let $X$ be a source domain not already reduced to ACP. A cross-domain intake record consists of:

- $B_X$: the candidate boundary pair in $X$; e.g. dissolution / crystallization analogues.
- $M_X$: the stabilizing or self-reinforcing mechanisms in $X$.
- $O_X$: the domain-native observables that could measure entropy, rank, variance, accessible modes, coordination floor, or inquiry-space.
- $\phi_X$: a proposed bridge map from $(B_X, M_X, O_X)$ into ACP objects.
- $F_X$: the failure set, i.e. which parts of the ACP mapping do not yet exist, are ambiguous, or appear false.
- $Q_X$: the successor questions produced by the mapping attempt.

The failure set is not an embarrassment. It is the main instrument. A domain improves the theory when $F_X$ is specific enough to generate $Q_X$.

## 3. Generativity Gain

Define the cross-domain generativity gain of an intake attempt as:

$$
G_X =
\frac{
|Q_X^{\mathrm{new}}| + |O_X^{\mathrm{new}}| + |P_X^{\mathrm{new}}|
}{
\max(1, |C_X|)
}.
$$

Here:

- $Q_X^{\mathrm{new}}$ is the set of new well-posed questions produced by the intake.
- $O_X^{\mathrm{new}}$ is the set of new measurable observables or bridge variables exposed by the domain.
- $P_X^{\mathrm{new}}$ is the set of new falsification paths, simulation routes, or empirical protocols.
- $C_X$ is the set of claims the intake closes or strengthens.

An intake is generative when $G_X > 1$. This is not evidence that the mapping is true. It is evidence that the encounter improves the research program rather than merely decorating it.

## 4. Promotion Ladder

Cross-domain material should move through the following statuses.

1. **Probe.** The domain suggests a boundary pair or mechanism, but no bridge map is stable yet. Home: `WHAT_IF.md`.
2. **Candidate bridge.** The domain supplies $B_X$, $M_X$, $O_X$, and a provisional $\phi_X$, with explicit $F_X$. Home: `bridges/`.
3. **Conditional extension.** The bridge yields a result under named hypotheses, with the hypotheses tracked as open problems.
4. **Reduction.** The domain result is recovered by variable identification plus a bridge lemma, matching the standard reduction pattern in A.11-A.15 and A.19.
5. **Theorem.** The ACP-side result no longer depends on the source domain and can stand in the proof chain.

No item should skip the ladder because it feels explanatory. The ladder is the restraint mechanism.

## 5. The Incompleteness Sensor

For a source domain $X$, the most valuable output is often not $\phi_X$ but $F_X$. The failure set should be classified as one of:

- **Observable gap.** The domain has the right boundary shape, but no measurable analogue of $H(m'|m)$, rank, coordination floor, or inquiry-space is known.
- **Partition gap.** The domain may instantiate ACP but lacks a natural subsystem or MASA-like partition, making A.20 unavailable.
- **Mechanism gap.** The boundary pair is clear, but the self-reinforcing mechanisms are not identified.
- **Conservation gap.** A coordination transfer appears present, but no conserved or kernel-preserving quantity has been stated.
- **Falsification gap.** The mapping is too elastic; it explains after the fact but does not yet predict.
- **Scope gap.** The domain exposes a class of systems outside the current ACP statement.

A gap becomes useful when it can be rewritten as a named open problem, a `WHAT_IF.md` entry, a simulation, or a falsifiable prediction.

For a more detailed diagnostic vocabulary, use `research/blind_spot_atlas_2026-04-24.md`, which refines these gaps into hidden carrier, hidden multiplicity, hidden transformation, hidden partition, hidden conservation law, hidden observable, hidden falsifier, hidden scope boundary, and hidden normative filter.

## 6. Historical Blind-Spot Audit

History is a special cross-domain source because it gives partial access to the structure of past ignorance. A later theory, instrument, or crisis often reveals that an earlier field did not merely lack an answer; it lacked the observable, partition, or vocabulary needed to ask the right question.

For a historical episode $H = (t_0, t_1)$, treat the source object as the transition from an earlier inquiry-space to a later one:

- $I_{t_0}$: the questions, observables, and distinctions available before the transition.
- $I_{t_1}$: the questions, observables, and distinctions available after the transition.
- $S_H = I_{t_1} \setminus I_{t_0}$: the blind-spot signature, i.e. what became askable only after the field learned more.
- $R_H$: the retrospective reconstruction of why those questions were invisible at $t_0$.

The point is not to judge the earlier field for failing to know the future. The point is to use the asymmetry between $t_0$ and $t_1$ as a map of how blind spots become visible. A unified theory improves itself when it asks: which present ACP gaps have the same shape as a past gap that later became measurable?

This gives a historical version of the failure set:

- **Instrumental blind spot.** The relevant observable existed physically but could not yet be measured.
- **Conceptual blind spot.** The field had measurements but lacked the category needed to interpret them.
- **Partition blind spot.** The field treated a composite system as undifferentiated, or imposed the wrong subsystem split.
- **Scale blind spot.** The important dynamics lived above or below the field's active scale of description.
- **Normative blind spot.** A community's standards of explanation excluded the very question that later became central.

Historical intake is generative when $S_H$ points to a present-day ACP failure set $F_X$ and turns it into a sharper observable, bridge map, or falsification path. In slogan form: we know that we do not know because later structure can make earlier absence visible. A unified theory should use that asymmetry deliberately.

## 7. Practical Intake Checklist

Before promoting an unexpected-domain idea beyond `WHAT_IF.md`, record:

- What are the two absorbing boundaries?
- What is the productive interval?
- What mechanism both preserves the system and risks driving it toward crystallization?
- What domain-native quantity could measure the relevant entropy, rank, variance, accessible-mode count, coordination floor, or inquiry-space?
- What would falsify the mapping?
- Which part fails to map cleanly?
- Does the failure open at least one sharper question than the one we started with?
- If the source is historical, what became askable at $t_1$ that was not askable at $t_0$, and which present ACP gap has the same shape?

The last question is the A.21 check. If the answer is no, the idea may still be interesting, but it has not yet improved the theory.

## 8. First Use

This protocol retroactively explains why `WHAT_IF.md` is not a side notebook. It is an adaptive coherence steering device. It keeps the theory in contact with domains whose partial fit can expose missing structure.

Immediate candidates for protocol-driven intake:

- **Language.** Existing entry WI-A.3 already has a candidate meaning-space mapping. The next step is to identify a measurable inquiry-space or expression-turnover observable.
- **Scientific disciplines.** WI-A.11 and WI-E.7 are direct OP-10 probes: they ask whether dominant theories crystallize downstream inquiry.
- **Model collapse.** WI-B.2 is a possible compression-bound route into OP-12.
- **Systems with no natural partition.** WI-E.3 is a partition-gap probe that may delimit A.20 without threatening the broader CDT. First bridge: `bridges/partition_generating_functors.md`.
- **Generative but wrong theories.** WI-E.4 stress-tests whether $G > 1$ is only a persistence criterion or can be tied to correctness.
- **History of science and institutions.** WI-F.6 and WI-G.4 use historical transitions as blind-spot audits: what later became measurable, sayable, or structurally necessary that the earlier regime could not yet pose? First pass: `research/historical_blind_spot_audit_2026-04-24.md`.

The working rule is simple: prefer the strange domain that creates a sharper measurement problem over the familiar domain that merely agrees.

## 9. Boundary Note

This protocol does not license arbitrary analogy. It does the opposite. It gives analogy a job and a burden: expose an observable, a bridge map, a falsification path, or a precise failure. Cross-domain knowledge improves ACP only when it increases the program's disciplined incompleteness.
